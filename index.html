01 / THE DISTILLED PLAYBOOK

Twelve principles worth returning to before every session.

Filtered from the conversations into practical language. No filler, no motivational noise—only ideas that change behaviour.

01✦

Your brain was built to survive, not to trade.

Uncertainty can feel like danger. A losing candle can activate the same protective machinery designed for physical threat—fight, flee, freeze or force control.

Translate it into actionNever make a new decision while your body feels threatened.
02◎

Performance beats winning.

You cannot command the next outcome. You can command preparation, risk, entry criteria and whether you obey the plan.

Wrong metricDid I win?
Useful metricDid I execute?
03⌁

Become an expert at losing.

A professional loss is planned, small and emotionally ordinary. The trader survives by cutting invalid ideas—not by defending identity.

  • Known invalidation
  • Predefined risk
  • No revenge entry
04◌

Emotion arrives before explanation.

Humans often feel first and rationalise second. The story that appears after a bad entry may simply be your emotion protecting itself.

“Is this trade in the plan—or am I building a reason because I already want it?”
05◉

Observe the observer.

Step behind the thought. Notice tension, shallow breathing, urgency and the need to be right before they become an order.

jaw or shoulder tension accelerated clicking bargaining with the stop
06≈

Regulate the body to recover the mind.

Slow diaphragmatic breathing is not decoration. It reduces arousal so the thinking brain can return to the decision.

07◇

Perfectionism is disguised fear.

Needing the perfect entry or refusing to be wrong increases anxiety, missed trades and rule-breaking. Probability demands room for imperfect outcomes.

One trade = noise. A large sample = evidence.
08↺

Winning can hijack you too.

Euphoria raises risk tolerance and creates the illusion of control. After a big win, the next task is not “press harder”—it is returning to baseline.

Dopamine risk: overconfidence → oversizing
09◷

Your cognitive capacity is finite.

Accuracy declines when attention is depleted. Use scheduled breaks before mistakes become visible in P&L.

00 prepare15 state check30 state check60 leave screen
10♡

Shame does not build discipline.

Self-attack strengthens the emotional pattern behind failure. Review mistakes with honesty and compassion: firm on behaviour, separate from worth.

“I am a terrible trader.”“I broke rule 3. Why?”
11▦

Trust must come from evidence.

Backtesting and journaling reduce the urge to interfere. A verified edge gives the mind permission to let probability work.

BacktestExecuteReviewTrust
12△

The chart is a mirror of human behaviour.

Price records collective fear, urgency, relief and greed. Reading structure becomes clearer when you stop demanding certainty from it.

See the market. Notice yourself. Execute the rule.

03 / 7-DAY TRAINING PROTOCOL

Knowledge becomes useful only after rehearsal.

A compact routine that converts podcast insight into embodied behaviour. Repeat weekly until the process feels ordinary.

DAY 01◉

Watch yourself

Record one session. Note breathing, posture, clicking speed and the moments your body changes.

DAY 02≈

Train the breath

Complete three five-minute sessions using a longer exhale than inhale.

DAY 03▦

Name the pattern

Review ten recent mistakes. Tag each as fear, greed, revenge, fatigue or rule ambiguity.

DAY 04◇

Define a good loss

Write the exact conditions that make a losing trade professionally executed.

DAY 05△

Choose your state

Select one symbol of discipline. Use posture, breath and language to rehearse that identity.

DAY 06◷

Trade in intervals

Run state checks every 15 minutes and leave the screen before 60–75 minutes of continuous focus.

DAY 07↺

Score the process

Grade preparation, rule adherence, risk and emotional regulation. Ignore money until the review is complete.
